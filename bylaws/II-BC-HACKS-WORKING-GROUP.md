# Bylaw II — BC Hacks Working Group

Bylaw II was adopted with the authority of the CSCU Executive Council on 2024-01-27.

## 0. Definitions

- The BCHWG - BC Hacks Working Group
- BC Hacks Project - all affairs related to BC Hacks

The key words "must", "must not", "required", "shall", "shall not", "should", "should not", "recommended",  "may", and "optional" in this document are to be interpreted as described in [RFC 2119](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc2119).

## 1. Summary

This bylaw describes the BC Hacks Working Group (the BCHWG) as a sub-circle of the CSCU, its responsibilities, and the CSCU's responsibilities to it.

The BCHWG is a semi-autonomous group associated with the CSCU whose primary responsibility is organizing an annual hackathon called BC Hacks and maintaining the BC Hacks brand.

## 2. Structure

The BCHWG is a sub-circle of the CSCU Executive Council, as described in Bylaw I.

The BCHWG must have at least one member and at least one member must be appointed by the CSCU Executive Council (the CSCU Representative). The CSCU Representative is appointed to represent the interests of the CSCU in the BC Hacks Project. The CSCU Representative is responsible to represent the interests of the whole CSCU Executive Council, and the CSCU Executive Council may counsel the CSCU Representative on how to fulfill their responsibility.

The CSCU Representative shall be responsible for all business that requires the authority of the CSCU as a student association (e.g. submitting reimbursements to the VP Finance of the CSCU and booking requests through the SUO).

The BCHWG should appoint other people to be members of the the BCHWG. Members need not necessarily be general members of the CSCU.

The BCHWG should have people fulfilling the following responsibilities:
- Lead Correspondent - A "point of contact" who is responsible for representing the interests of the BCHWG with other organizations (e.g. MLH)
- Sponsorship Coordinator - One who solicits sponsorships for the BC Hacks project
- Communications Lead - One who creates and shares media in promotion of the BC Hacks project and manages its brand
- Secretary - One who runs meetings and otherwise manages the internal affairs of the BCHWG

Members of the BCHWG may occupy more than one role as required, however The BCHWG should aim to appoint at least enough members to fill the four roles listed above. The BCHWG may appoint as many members as it decides is necessary.

All members excluding the CSCU Representative of the the BCHWG may remain members of the the BCHWG so long as:
- They want to
- Two-thirds (⅔) of the BCHWG don't decide to remove them from the team.

The CSCU Representative serves as the CSCU Representative from the time they are appointed until the end of the subsequent CSCU election. The CSCU Executive Council may decide to replace the CSCU Representative at will. The newly elected team of the CSCU shall be responsible for appointing a new CSCU Representative.

Decisions in the BCHWG shall be made according to Bylaw I of the CSCU.

## 3. Relationship with the CSCU

Formally, the BCHWG is an extension of the CSCU. The BCHWG must not violate any rules put forth by SUO bylaws or regulations.

The BCHWG must not hold any money independently. All finances shall be conducted through the CSCU. The CSCU shall be the custodian of money belonging to the BCHWG. The CSCU must not use money explicitly allocated to the BCHWG without the authorization of the BCHWG.

The CSCU must apply for funding from the SUO for BC Hacks during Winter Term 1 or Winter Term 2, depending on when the main, annual BC Hacks event is taking place.

The CSCU may, with the approval of the BCHWG, seek ratification for the BCHWG as an SUO club unto itself. If the BCHWG is to be ratified as a club, it shall be subject to a modification of this bylaw as adopted by both the CSCU and the BCHWG.

The CSCU may host the CSCU AGM during either the opening ceremony or closing ceremony of BC Hacks at its own discretion.

The CSCU shall not impede on the operations of the BCHWG conducted through the CSCU Representative except when compelled to by the SUO or CSCU general membership, as resolved during a CSCU general meeting.

## 4. Disbandment

The BCHWG may be disbanded for a term if the BCHWG decides it should be disbanded.

If the BCHWG is dispanded, all digital assets essential to running and preserving history related to the BC Hacks project should be archived and placed under the the stewardship of the CSCU Executive Council. The CSCU Executive Council must preserve this archive and pass it to future leadership. Assets include but are not limited to:
- Budgets
- Branding material
- Sponsorship packages
- Website assets

If the BCHWG is disbanded before a CSCU general election, the new CSCU Executive Council may attempt to recreate the BCHWG by appointing a new CSCU Representative and seeking applications for members.

The CSCU Executive Council may decide to use remaining BCHWG funds for purposes unrelated to the BC Hacks project if the BCHWG decides to disband itself and one complete winter academic session has passed with absolutely no BCHWG activity. In other words, two consecutive CSCU Executive Councils must decide that BCHWG should remain disbanded.

If the BCHWG is disbanded, then the CSCU Executive Council shall have no responsibility to fulfill the obligations of Bylaw II.
